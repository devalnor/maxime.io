## Problem Statement

Maxime.io still presents a 2020-era professional profile. Its business/design/technology framing contains a durable truth, but the copy, technology inventory and professional evidence no longer represent Maxime's current practice. The update must establish a memorable senior profile for peers and potential startup clients without turning the site into a job-seeking page or an AI-generated portfolio template.

## What Makes This Cool

The site itself already has an authored identity: the black opening fade, portrait-led composition, restrained typography and original animated SVGs. The opportunity is to preserve that artifact while changing what it communicates. The updated page should make a long record of learning visible: from the early web and technical presales through UX architecture, product engineering and current AI experimentation.

## Constraints

- Keep the current visual language, opening fade, original SVG animations and restrained one-page rhythm.
- Avoid AI-portfolio conventions: no bento grid, decorative badges, generic gradient spectacle, personal chatbot, inflated slogans or animation without a UX purpose.
- Write the public site in English.
- Position Maxime as `Creative Technologist & UX Architect`, with senior front-end development as a core part of the practice rather than a footnote.
- Demonstrate seniority through professional evolution and judgment, not a prominent `25+ years` claim.
- Treat Mbrella as a client and a recent long-term freelance mission, never as Maxime's identity or entire career.
- Preserve the breadth of the Emakina years and earlier work without claiming sole authorship of agency projects.
- Present L'Inférence with a direct link inside an extensible `Lab` section. It is an AI editorial experiment, not a media venture seeking search traffic.
- Leave room in the content strategy for occasional substantive writing, expected to be only a few articles per year; publishing support itself is explicitly deferred from this release.
- Make the same factual content legible to humans, search engines and AI agents; do not create a separate marketing narrative for machines.
- Keep content visible by default. JavaScript may enhance reveals only after initialization; a failed or disabled script must never leave copy or links hidden.
- Preserve keyboard access, visible focus, meaningful alternatives and a complete reduced-motion state, including the opening fade, SVG drawing and terminal cursor.
- Do not add AI authorship or co-author trailers to project commits.

## Premises

- The site's primary job is durable personal branding: visitors should remember a rare, credible and versatile profile for a future need.
- `Creative Technologist & UX Architect` is the narrative spine. UX architecture, senior front-end engineering, mobile, R&D, IoT and AI are evidence of that practice.
- `Couteau suisse` is an internal truth, not the headline. Breadth needs a clear organizing idea to remain credible.
- Creativity comes before product management. The copy must not imply a Product Owner or people-management ambition.
- A small number of truthful, specific examples is stronger than a large skills or logo inventory.
- The career is best understood in periods because the repeated pattern is adaptation and learning as technologies change.
- Mbrella provides depth; the Emakina period and organization archive provide breadth; Lab provides independently owned experimentation.
- Machine readability should come from semantic HTML, explicit relationships, descriptive metadata and structured data, not from visible SEO filler.
- Release copy and metadata must be generated from one user-approved fact sheet; unapproved claims and confidential details do not ship.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Conservative Revision

Keep the existing section order and add new material. Rejected because the 2020 skills and technology inventories would continue to dominate the story.

### Approach B: Evidence Before Inventory

Preserve the existing experience but reorganize the content around a point of view, professional evolution and concrete evidence. Selected because it modernizes the meaning without imposing a new aesthetic.

### Approach C: Editorial Multipage Site

Split the profile into Work, Lab, Notes and About pages. Deferred because it would change the nature of the current site and require more content and maintenance than the present publishing cadence justifies.

## Recommended Approach

Use Approach B as a restrained evolution of the current one-page site.

### Content architecture

1. **Hero and point of view** — Name, `Creative Technologist & UX Architect`, a headline of at most 16 words and an introduction of at most 80 words connecting creativity, UX and engineering. The copy should sound like Maxime, not like a consultancy proposition.
2. **A practice shaped by change** — Editorial introduction of at most 70 words explaining that the technologies and roles have evolved while curiosity, learning and the ability to connect disciplines have remained constant.
3. **Professional periods** — Small typographic sections, not a timeline component:
   - `Building for the early web` — development, CMS work, early digital platforms and entrepreneurship.
   - `Connecting technology and business` — technical presales, new business and client guidance.
   - `Designing digital experiences` — strategy, information architecture, UX, interfaces and R&D across thirteen years with Emakina.
   - `Shaping a product at scale` — the current long-term freelance mission for Mbrella.
   - `Exploring what comes next` — a bridge to Lab, without repeating individual projects.
   Each period gets a short heading and at most 55 words centered on what changed, what Maxime learned or how the practice expanded. Dates and duties are secondary. The Mbrella period receives only a one-sentence summary here; evidence belongs in the next section.
4. **Recent client work: Mbrella** — A clearly attributed example of at most 180 words covering the mobility-budget HR web app, mobile application and broader AI tooling. Describe Maxime's contribution without appropriating company-wide metrics.
5. **Practice areas** — Reuse the three original animated SVGs and their existing layout under their truthful existing concepts:
   - `Business`: customer experience, technical presales, digital strategy and client collaboration.
   - `Design`: UX architecture, information architecture, interaction design and training.
   - `Technology`: senior front-end development, mobile, prototyping/R&D and AI/IoT exploration.
   Use three or four short items per area. The illustrations must be visually reviewed at desktop and mobile before reuse is accepted.
6. **Lab** — A simple list driven by data, not a new card system. Each entry has a title, year or status, description of at most 60 words, external URL and optional image. Launch with L'Inférence linking directly to `https://linference.be`; if no suitable image is approved, the entry remains text-only. Ownership wording must say it is a personal experiment.
7. **Experience breadth** — Retain the existing organization and sector archive as a deliberately secondary historical index, distinct from the small number of detailed examples. Place one global attribution directly below the heading: `Selected organizations I worked with, including work delivered as part of agency teams.` Every retained or new organization name must appear in the approved fact sheet.
8. **Contact** — Keep a two-line maximum, permanent invitation to exchange ideas or discuss a problem. Avoid `available for work` language.

`Notes` is deferred from this release. No empty section or publishing infrastructure ships before the first article is approved. When that happens, its routes, archive, dates, metadata, sitemap and optional feed will receive a separate small design decision.

### Content and machine-readable structure

- Keep important claims as visible server-rendered HTML.
- Use one clear page title and description aligned with the visible positioning.
- Use semantic heading order and descriptive links.
- Add one JSON-LD `@graph`: a `ProfilePage` node with `@id`, canonical `url`, `name`, `description`, `inLanguage: en` and `mainEntity`; and a `Person` node with `@id`, `name`, `url`, approved portrait `image`, `jobTitle`, approved LinkedIn/GitHub `sameAs` links and a short approved `knowsAbout` list. Do not use `worksFor` for Mbrella and do not model Lab projects in structured data in phase one.
- Keep sitemap, canonical URL, Open Graph data and robots directives consistent.
- Do not add keyword lists, hidden prose or invented authority signals for AI agents.

### Layout, accessibility and verification

- Reuse the current `1200px` content maximum and existing breakpoints at `1024px`, `900/769px` and `768px`; do not introduce a parallel layout system.
- New editorial sections use the existing content column. Below `768px`, they stack in source order with no new horizontal scrolling. The existing organization carousel behavior may remain.
- Keep the current navigation limited to Contact and external social links for this release; do not add anchors for every section.
- Render all text and links visible before JavaScript runs. Add an enhancement class only after script initialization, then apply reveal states beneath that class.
- At `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, remove the opening fade transition, portrait/reveal motion, every SVG stroke animation and the terminal cursor blink; show each element in its completed state.
- Treat this as component-level work: audit and, where necessary, edit the embedded animation rules in each of the three SVG components, then verify that every illustration has a complete and legible static state.
- Preserve semantic landmarks and sequential headings; all interactive elements must be reachable by keyboard with a visible focus state. Decorative SVGs remain hidden from assistive technology; linked project text must name its destination.
- Treat the portrait as informative and expose `Portrait of Maxime de Visscher` as its accessible name. Treat a Lab preview as decorative when its adjacent text conveys the same information; if it communicates additional project content, give it a concise approved alternative.
- Review at `1440x900`, `1024x768`, `768x1024` and `390x844`, plus JavaScript-disabled and reduced-motion modes. Run Astro check/build, validate the JSON-LD shape, and check every internal and external link. The release adds no client-side framework and no new JavaScript dependency.

- A reader cannot reasonably conclude that Maxime works for, represents or is defined solely by Mbrella.
- The Emakina period, earlier career and breadth of client exposure remain visible.
- Every named project and organization has clear, honest attribution.
- The page demonstrates evolution from early web work to current AI experimentation without reading like a chronological CV.
- L'Inférence appears as the first item in a Lab structure that supports additional projects.
- No Notes navigation, empty state or unused publishing system appears before the first approved article.
- Maxime gives explicit visual sign-off after comparing the finished preview side by side with `sept-2026`; any new pattern without a stated content or interaction purpose is removed.
- With JavaScript disabled, every content section and link remains visible and usable.
- Reduced-motion mode contains no opening fade, reveal, SVG drawing or cursor-blink animation.
- At the four review viewports, new sections remain in source order, text does not overflow and no new horizontal scroll is introduced.
- Keyboard traversal reaches every link with a visible focus state; heading levels remain sequential.
- Astro checks and production build pass, all links resolve, and the JSON-LD contains the approved `ProfilePage` and `Person` nodes without a false Mbrella employment relationship.
